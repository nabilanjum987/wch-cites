## The Story

Christ the Redeemer (Cristo Redentor) is a colossal Art Deco statue of Jesus standing 709 metres up Corcovado mountain, arms open over Rio de Janeiro. The statue itself is 30 metres tall on an 8-metre pedestal, with an arm span of 28 metres, built of reinforced concrete and clad in six million soapstone tiles applied by hand.

It was designed by Brazilian engineer Heitor da Silva Costa with French sculptor Paul Landowski, funded overwhelmingly by donations from Brazilian Catholics, built in Brazil between 1922 and 1931, and inaugurated on 12 October 1931. In 2007 it was voted one of the New7Wonders of the World; UNESCO inscribed the Carioca Landscapes between the Mountain and the Sea (site 1100) in 2012 — the listing that frames the statue's mountain setting.

## Visitor guide

**Best time.** Access runs daily from the morning; the plaza closes in the evening and the summit closes entirely in dangerous weather. Corcovado makes its own weather — the statue disappears into cloud while the beaches below sit in sun — so the live weather block on the page, sampled at the statue's coordinates, is the first thing to check before buying a ticket up. Operators publish the day's times.

**How to reach.** There is no public road to the summit for private cars. Go by the Corcovado cog train from Cosme Velho, or by official vans from the visitor centres (Paineiras, Largo do Machado, Copacabana); train and van tickets include the ascent. Buy through the official operators (Trem do Corcovado) to avoid the informal sellers at the Cosme Velho station. Fees are deliberately not printed here — the packaged data row carries none verified.

## What to wear (framework — keyed to the live weather layer)

Read the live conditions block first: summit cloud is the deciding factor. In clear heat — light clothing, hat, sunscreen, water; the plaza is exposed. If the live block shows cloud or rain at the summit, expect wind and a sharp temperature drop at 709 m — carry a layer and a waterproof shell. Footwear with grip: the plaza and steps are slick when wet.

## Myth vs Fact

**Myth: The statue was a gift from France. — PARTLY TRUE.** The design has French fingerprints — Landowski modelled it and parts were fabricated in France — but it was conceived in Brazil, engineered by da Silva Costa, built in Brazil and paid for by Brazilian Catholic donations. *(Myth: popular shorthand; correction: Wikipedia — "Christ the Redeemer (statue)", as recorded in the data row.)*

**Myth: Lightning never dares strike the statue. — FALSE.** Corcovado's summit is struck repeatedly — several times a year by most counts. A January 2014 strike broke the tip of a finger on the right hand; restorers periodically replace damaged soapstone tiles and maintain the lightning-protection system. *(2014 restoration reports, as recorded in the data row.)*
