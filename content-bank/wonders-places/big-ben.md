## The Story

"Big Ben" is the Great Bell of the clock tower at the north end of the Palace of Westminster in London — and, by universal usage, the name of the tower itself (officially the Elizabeth Tower since 2012). The packaged facts: the tower was built in the Gothic Revival rebuilding of Parliament, construction starting 28 September 1843, designed by Charles Barry with Augustus Pugin shaping its Gothic detail, built of Caen stone and limestone, and standing 96 metres tall. The clock and its bell became the sound of London — broadcast live, imitated worldwide, and so embedded that the tower needs no other introduction on the London skyline.

## Visitor guide

**How to visit.** Big Ben is primarily an exterior landmark: the tower is viewed from Westminster Bridge, Parliament Square and the Thames paths — the classic frame is from the bridge at the hour. The Elizabeth Tower is part of the UK Parliament estate; Parliament runs guided tours of the Palace of Westminster, and tower tours (which climb to the bell) are offered on a limited basis to UK residents through their MP — the current arrangements, and the general visitor programme, are published on the UK Parliament website (parliament.uk). Do not rely on any third-party "Big Ben ticket" seller: there is no general public ticket office for the tower.

**Best time.** The chimes and the floodlit tower after dark are the two experiences; the Westminster area is at its busiest mid-day (see the London city file in B1).
