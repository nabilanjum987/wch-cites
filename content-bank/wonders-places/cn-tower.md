## The Story

The CN Tower is Toronto's needle — a 553.3-metre concrete-and-steel communications and observation tower (packaged height and materials), designed by John Andrews and completed in 1976 (the packaged inception year, its opening era). For more than three decades it was the world's tallest free-standing structure, and it remains the defining point of the Toronto skyline and the anchor landmark of the Toronto city file in this bank: a working broadcast tower that doubles as the city's observation deck, with the glass floor and the SkyPod looking down the height that made it famous, and Lake Ontario spread out below.

## Visitor guide

**How to visit.** The tower stands in downtown Toronto, beside the Rogers Centre and Ripley's Aquarium, a short walk from Union Station. Visits are by ticket sold through the tower's official site (cntower.ca) — timed general admission to the observation levels, with the higher SkyPod and the EdgeWalk (a hands-free walk on the main pod's outer rim, run by the operator in season) as separate products. Current prices, hours and availability are on the official site and are deliberately not printed here. Book ahead in summer and on weekends.

**Best time.** Clear days are everything at 553 metres — check the live weather block on the page; a cloud-base below the pod makes the deck a white-out. Sunset slots, city day turning to city lights, are the most sought after.

## What to wear (framework — keyed to the live weather layer)

Read the live conditions block first. Toronto's winter is the extreme case: the observation levels sit in serious wind chill — dress for the street temperature, not the indoor queue, if you plan any outdoor deck time. In summer, the deck is sun-exposed: sunscreen and a hat for the outdoor terrace. EdgeWalk participants are issued weather gear by the operator — follow their kit rules, not this file.
