## The Story

The Golden Gate Bridge is San Francisco's orange span across the strait where the bay meets the Pacific. The packaged facts: designed under Joseph Strauss, built of steel, and opened in 1937 (the packaged date, 27 May 1937 — its pedestrian opening). Its Art Deco towers and International Orange paint made engineering into icon: a suspension bridge designed to flex in the strait's winds and tides, and painted the colour of visibility in the fog that defines it as often as the sun does.

## Visitor guide

**How to visit.** The bridge is walkable and cyclable: pedestrian access is on the east walkway, reached from the San Francisco-side visitor plaza (with the Welcome Center) or from Vista Point on the Marin side. Walking the full span and back is the classic visit. Fog is the bridge's weather personality — the live weather block on the page decides whether you get the postcard or the mystery, and both are authentic. Current walkway hours and any works closures are published by the Golden Gate Bridge District (goldengate.org) — check there before a dawn or evening walk.

**Best time.** Morning on a clear day, before the afternoon wind and fog build; the light then belongs to photographers, not this file.

## What to wear (framework — keyed to the live weather layer)

Read the live conditions block first. The strait is a wind tunnel at bay level in every month: a windproof layer is mandatory kit, and in San Francisco's summer — famously cold on the water — wear winter-weight layers while the rest of California wears shorts. Shoes with grip for damp deck plates.
