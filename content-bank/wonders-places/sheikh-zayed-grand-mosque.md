## The Story

The Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque is Abu Dhabi's crown — the largest mosque in the United Arab Emirates and the centrepiece of the capital's landmark set in this bank. The packaged facts: designed by Yusef Abdelki, in a style the row describes as Mughal and Moorish architecture, and opened in 2007 (the packaged date, 20 December 2007 — its opening era). White domes and minarets rise over a courtyard of floral marble inlay, between Abu Dhabi's bridges at the entrance to the island city. It was commissioned by Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan, the UAE's founding president, whose tomb stands in its courtyard — a national monument as much as a house of worship.

## Visitor guide

**How to visit.** The mosque is the principal landmark of the Abu Dhabi city file in B1. It is a working mosque that also runs one of the Gulf's most organised visitor programmes: visitors register in advance through the mosque's official visitor portal (the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque Centre), follow a designated visitor route, and join free guided tours run by the Centre; visits are made outside prayer times, and Friday patterns differ. Current registration, tour times and Ramadan arrangements are published by the Centre — check the official portal before going rather than relying on any printed guide, including this file.

**Best time.** Late afternoon into evening: the white marble cools, the domes take the sunset, and the mosque's night lighting is a second building entirely.
