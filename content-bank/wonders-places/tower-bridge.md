## The Story

Tower Bridge is London's operatic river crossing — a steel-and-concrete bascule bridge (packaged materials) dressed in Gothic Revival stone (packaged style), designed under Horace Jones, the City Architect, with construction beginning in 1886 (packaged inception). Its twin towers rise 65 metres (packaged height) over the Thames beside the Tower of London — the pairing that produces both its name and the world's most common London confusion: visitors buy "London Bridge" and photograph this one. The high-level walkways between the towers, built so pedestrians could cross while the bascules opened for ships, are now its exhibition space.

## Visitor guide

**How to visit.** The bridge carries road and pedestrian traffic across the Thames and can be walked free at street level, day and night — the classic view is from the south bank near City Hall or from the Tower of London side. Going inside is the Tower Bridge Exhibition: the towers, the glass-floored high-level walkways and the Victorian engine rooms, ticketed through the official Tower Bridge site (towerbridge.org.uk), which also publishes bridge-lift times — the moments the roadway still rises for tall ships. Current prices and opening times are on the official site and are deliberately not printed here.

**Best time.** A scheduled bridge lift, if your day allows one; otherwise the blue hour, when the bridge lights against the river (see the London city file in B1).
