## The Story

The Giza pyramid complex is the last surviving wonder of the ancient world: three pyramid complexes built by the Fourth-Dynasty kings Khufu, Khafre and Menkaure around 2600–2500 BC, guarded by the Great Sphinx and ringed by queens' pyramids, mastaba tombs, causeways and valley temples on the edge of the Sahara.

The Great Pyramid of Khufu — 146.6 metres when new, 138.8 metres today — was the tallest human-made structure on Earth for more than 3,800 years, and its roughly 2.3 million stone blocks still anchor arguments about exactly how Egypt did it. What is no longer argued is who did it: the excavated workers' town, bakeries, breweries and the builders' own tombs, plus the diary of an inspector named Merer — the oldest inscribed papyrus — document a paid, rotating Egyptian workforce.

UNESCO inscribed "Memphis and its Necropolis — the Pyramid Fields from Giza to Dahshur" as World Heritage Site 86 in 1979.

## Visitor guide

**Best time.** The plateau opens daily in the morning and closes before sunset; hours shift between summer and winter seasons. Morning visits beat both the heat and the crowds. Inner-pyramid entry is by separate ticket with limited daily numbers. Egypt's Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities (Egymonuments) publishes the current schedule and prices — fees are deliberately not printed here; the packaged data row carries none verified.

**How to reach.** Most visitors enter through the gates by the Great Pyramid or the Sphinx-side entrance. The plateau is large and exposed — check the live weather block on the page (heat, sun, wind-blown sand) before setting out.

## What to wear (framework — keyed to the live weather layer)

Read the live conditions block first. Giza is an exposed desert plateau: in heat (the common case), light breathable clothing that covers shoulders and knees (both sun protection and site etiquette), a hat, sunscreen and substantial water. In the cooler winter band, a jacket for early mornings and the plateau wind. Footwear with grip in all seasons — the going is sand, rubble and worn stone, and pyramid interiors are steep and confined.

## Myth vs Fact

**Myth: The pyramids were built by slave labour. — FALSE.** The Giza workers' town had industrial bakeries, breweries and animal bones in quantities that fed thousands of paid workers, and the builders' own tombs stand in the pyramids' shadow — an honour no slave received. Egyptologists describe a rotating national workforce, fed and housed by the state. *(Myth: later legend; correction: Egyptologists Mark Lehner & Zahi Hawass — Giza Plateau Mapping Project.)*

**Myth: Aliens built the pyramids. — FALSE.** The construction record is Egyptian and paper-based: Inspector Merer's diary (the Wadi al-Jarf papyri) logs his crew hauling Tura limestone by boat to Khufu's pyramid, and quarry marks, tools and ramps document the methods on site. *(Wadi al-Jarf papyri, published 2013.)*

**Myth: The Great Pyramid deliberately encodes π and the Earth's dimensions. — PARTLY TRUE.** The slope follows the Egyptian seked system, which happens to produce a perimeter-to-height ratio close to 2π; Egyptologists treat the near-π value as a by-product of the chosen slope, not a coded constant. *(Egyptological consensus, as recorded in the data row.)*
