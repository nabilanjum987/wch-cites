## The Story

Stonehenge is a prehistoric ring of standing stones on Salisbury Plain in Wiltshire, England — the most architecturally sophisticated surviving stone circle in the world. Its sarsen stones, capped by lintels in a unique jointed construction, were raised around 2500 BC; the circle sits inside earthworks begun centuries earlier, around 3000 BC, and the monument was built and remodelled in stages down to about 2000 BC.

The logistics still stagger: the bluestones came from the Preseli Hills of Wales, some 240 km away, while the great sarsens — averaging 25 tonnes — were sourced from West Woods near Marlborough, about 25 km north, a provenance settled by geological fingerprinting only in 2020. Stonehenge is part of a dense ritual landscape of avenues, barrows and cursus monuments, aligned on the solstices — the Avenue points to midsummer sunrise and midwinter sunset. UNESCO inscribed Stonehenge with Avebury as site 373 in 1986; it is owned by the Crown and managed by English Heritage.

## Visitor guide

**Best time.** Book a timed slot online in advance — English Heritage is explicit that advance booking is the way to guarantee entry, and peak-season slots sell out. Early slots give the quietest circle and the best light. Allow about two hours for the visit.

**How to reach.** Start at the visitor centre, about 2 km from the stones (exhibition, café, Neolithic houses); a shuttle bus included in the ticket runs between the centre and the monument, or you can walk the approach. By public transport: train from London Waterloo to Salisbury, then the Stonehenge Tour bus from the station. Standard entry keeps you on the perimeter path around the stones; the special Stone Circle Access sessions, outside normal hours, are the only way inside the circle. Prices are deliberately not printed here — English Heritage's site carries the current figures.

## What to wear (framework — keyed to the live weather layer)

Read the live conditions block on the page first. Salisbury Plain is windswept and flat in every direction: a windproof layer in every season, full waterproofs when the live block shows rain — there is no shelter at the stones themselves (all facilities are at the visitor centre). In winter, add serious warmth; in summer, sun protection for an exposed walk with no shade.

## Myth vs Fact

**Myth: The Druids built Stonehenge. — FALSE.** The monument was already some 2,000 years old when Druids appear in the historical record. The Druid link was invented by 17th–18th-century antiquarians — though modern Druids do celebrate there today. *(Myth: antiquarian tradition; correction: Wikipedia — "Stonehenge", as recorded in the data row.)*

**Myth: Merlin raised the stones by magic. — FALSE.** Geoffrey of Monmouth's 12th-century tale has Merlin move the stones from Ireland for King Arthur's uncle. The legend has a kernel: the bluestones really did come from the far west — Wales, not Ireland, and by human hauling. *(Geoffrey of Monmouth; geological provenance studies.)*

**Myth: Nobody knows what it was for at all. — PARTLY TRUE.** Its solstice alignment and cremation cemetery are firmly evidenced, so "ceremonial and burial landscape aligned on the sun" is solid ground — but the full meaning to its builders is genuinely unrecoverable. *(English Heritage / scholarly consensus, as recorded in the data row.)*
