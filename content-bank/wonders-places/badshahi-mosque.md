## The Story

The Badshahi Mosque is Lahore's imperial mosque — built under the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb, with construction beginning in 1671 (packaged inception). It is Mughal architecture at full scale: red sandstone (the packaged material) inlaid with marble, three great domes and minarets rising 53.75 metres (packaged height), facing the Lahore Fort across the Hazuri Bagh. For centuries it stood among the largest mosques in the world, and it remains the defining silhouette of the Walled City's fort precinct — the ceremonial heart of the live Lahore city pages elsewhere on this site.

## Visitor guide

**How to visit.** The mosque stands in the fort precinct of Lahore's Walled City, opposite the Lahore Fort, and is reached through the Hazuri Bagh. It is a working mosque: visiting follows the prayer rhythm — tourists visit outside prayer times, remove shoes at the courtyard edge, and dress modestly (head covering for women; shoulders and knees covered for all). As a place of worship it does not run a ticket system of the museum kind; any current visiting arrangements are posted at the mosque and by the Auqaf Department, Punjab — check on the day rather than trusting any printed schedule, including this one.

**Best time.** Early morning or the hour before sunset, when the sandstone holds the light and the courtyard is coolest — Lahore's summer midday is the wrong hour for an open marble-and-sandstone courtyard (see the live weather block on the page).

## What to wear (framework — keyed to the live weather layer)

Read the live conditions block first, then the mosque rules: modest dress for all visitors — shoulders and knees covered; women cover their hair. Footwear comes off inside, so in Lahore's summer heat expect very hot stone underfoot in the courtyard at midday (another reason for the morning/evening rule); in winter, warm layers for an open courtyard at dawn prayers' end.
