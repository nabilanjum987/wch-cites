## The Story

Burj Khalifa is, at 828 metres with 163 floors, the tallest building and tallest free-standing structure ever built. Designed by Adrian Smith of Skidmore, Owings & Merrill, it rose between 2004 and 2009 and opened on 4 January 2010 — renamed at the last moment from Burj Dubai for Khalifa bin Zayed, ruler of Abu Dhabi, whose government had supported Dubai through the 2009 financial crisis.

The plan is a three-lobed spiral drawn from the desert spider lily and Islamic geometry: three wings step back around a central core, narrowing as the tower climbs so the wind is "confused" rather than organised into dangerous vortices. It anchors Downtown Dubai — the Dubai Mall, the Dubai Fountain, and the At The Top observation decks on levels 124, 125 and 148.

Burj Khalifa is not a UNESCO site; it is younger than some of its visitors' phones. But it already holds a shelf of records beyond height: highest occupied floor, highest observation deck, highest restaurant.

## Visitor guide

**Best time.** The At The Top decks open daily from morning to late evening; the sunset "prime" hours carry different prices and sell out first. The operator publishes the current schedule and dynamic prices on the official ticket site — nothing about deck pricing is fixed enough to print honestly, and the packaged data row carries no verified fee.

**How to reach.** Entry is through the Dubai Mall (lower ground floor, At The Top entrance) — not through the tower's own doors. Timed tickets are scanned at the mall entrance; allow time for security and the exhibition corridor before the lifts.

## Myth vs Fact

**Myth: People on the top floors must break their Ramadan fast later than people at the bottom. — TRUE.** The sun genuinely sets minutes later at 828 m. Dubai's Islamic affairs authorities issued guidance that residents above roughly the 80th floor should delay breaking their fast by about two minutes, and those above the 150th by about three. *(Myth and correction both documented: Dubai Islamic Affairs guidance, as recorded in the data row.)*

**Myth: Burj Khalifa can be seen from 95 km away. — PARTLY TRUE.** On exceptionally clear days the silhouette is reported visible from around 95 km — a figure the developer itself has used — but ordinary haze cuts practical visibility to a fraction of that. *(Emaar statements, as recorded in the data row.)*
