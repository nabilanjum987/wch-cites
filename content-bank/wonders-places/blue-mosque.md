## The Story

The Sultan Ahmed Mosque — the Blue Mosque of every traveller's vocabulary — is Istanbul's great imperial mosque, built under Sultan Ahmed I with construction beginning in 1609 (packaged inception), designed by Sedefkâr Mehmed Âğâ, architect of the Ottoman classical school. Its packaged facts give its measure: Ottoman architecture, minarets and domes rising to 64 metres. It faces the Hagia Sophia (banked separately in this set) across Sultanahmet Square — the two buildings are the paired poles of the old city's skyline, one Byzantine, one Ottoman, each answering the other. The "blue" of its name comes from the İznik tiles that line its interior.

## Visitor guide

**How to visit.** The Blue Mosque is a working mosque in Sultanahmet, Istanbul. Visitors enter outside the five daily prayer times, when the mosque closes to sightseeing; Friday midday is the longest closure. Entry for visitors is free as a place of worship, through the designated visitor entrance; the current visiting hours and arrangements are posted by the mosque and the Istanbul Müftülüğü — check on the day. It pairs naturally with the Hagia Sophia across the square, whose separate visitor system is described in that file.

**Best time.** Early morning, before the tour groups, or between afternoon and evening prayers — and note the prayer-time pattern shifts with the calendar, which is what the prayer layer of this site exists to show.

## What to wear (framework — keyed to the live weather layer)

Read the live conditions block first, then the mosque rules, which apply in all weather: modest dress — shoulders and knees covered for everyone; women cover their hair (scarves are traditionally available at the entrance). Shoes are removed inside. In Istanbul's winter, the stone floors are cold — thick socks are the local wisdom; in summer, light clothing that still meets the dress code.
