## The Story

The Grand Canyon is a mile-deep gorge carved by the Colorado River through the Colorado Plateau in northern Arizona: 446 km long, up to 29 km wide, and up to 1,857 m deep. Its exposed rock layers read like a book of Earth's history, from 270-million-year-old Kaibab limestone at the rim down to Vishnu schist at the river dated near 1.8 billion years.

The canyon sits within Grand Canyon National Park — protected first as a national monument by Theodore Roosevelt in 1908 (he called it "the one great sight which every American should see") and as a national park from 1919. UNESCO inscribed the park in 1979 (site 75). The canyon is the ancestral homeland of 11 Native American tribes with historic associations, including the Havasupai and Hualapai — the latter operate the Skywalk glass bridge on tribal land west of the park boundary. Most visitors see the canyon from the South Rim, open all year; the higher, quieter North Rim is seasonal.

## Visitor guide

**The two rims.** The **South Rim** is open 24 hours a day, year-round, and holds the developed visitor infrastructure — Grand Canyon Village, Mather Point, and the free shuttle-bus system (including the Kaibab Rim and Hermits Rest routes; Hermit Road is shuttle-only for much of the year). The **North Rim** is open seasonally, mid-May to mid-October, and closes to vehicles in winter. The West Rim and its Skywalk are not part of the national park — they are Hualapai tribal land, separately ticketed, and park passes do not apply there.

**How to reach.** South Rim entrances: the South Entrance via Highway 64 from Williams, and the East Entrance at Desert View via US-89 from near Flagstaff. The Grand Canyon Railway runs from Williams to the South Rim depot. The park entrance pass is valid for seven days for both rims; current fees are on the NPS site and are deliberately not printed here. The park's own motto for anyone going below the rim deserves quoting to every visitor: *down is optional, up is mandatory* — and there is no water below the rim except where the NPS lists it.

## What to wear (framework — keyed to the live weather layer)

Read the live conditions block on the page first — and read it for the right place: rim weather and inner-canyon weather are different climates. The South Rim sits near 2,100 m: cold and snowy in winter, mild in summer — layers and a wind shell for viewpoints. Below the rim, temperatures climb steeply (the river corridor can run far hotter than the rim on the same day): for any descent, sun hat, sunscreen, and carried water measured in litres, not sips. In the summer monsoon, lightning on exposed rim points is a real and recurring danger — move off the rim when storms build.

## Myth vs Fact

**Myth: It is the deepest canyon in the world. — FALSE.** At 1,857 m the Grand Canyon is not the record holder: Nepal's Kali Gandaki gorge and the Yarlung Tsangpo gorge in Tibet are deeper. The Grand Canyon's claim is scale, colour and exposed geology — not the depth title. *(Myth: popular shorthand; correction: Wikipedia — "Grand Canyon", as recorded in the data row.)*

**Myth: The Colorado River carved it over billions of years. — FALSE.** Parts of the rock are billions of years old, but the canyon itself is young: mainstream evidence puts most carving in the last 5–6 million years — the river cuts fast when the land rises under it. *(Wikipedia — "Grand Canyon".)*

**Myth: You can see it from the Moon. — FALSE.** Astronauts can pick it out from low Earth orbit in good light, but from the Moon — a thousand times farther — the canyon is far below the limit of the naked eye. *(Wikipedia — "Grand Canyon".)*
