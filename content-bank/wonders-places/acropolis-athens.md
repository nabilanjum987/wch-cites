## The Story

The Acropolis is a limestone rock rising about 156 metres above sea level in the centre of Athens, crowned by the most influential buildings of classical antiquity. Its centrepiece, the Parthenon, was built between 447 and 432 BC to designs by Iktinos and Kallikrates as the temple of Athena Parthenos, the city's patron goddess, its sculpture programme supervised under Pericles' great rebuilding of the temples the Persians had burned.

The Periclean programme also raised the Propylaea gateway, the Erechtheion with its famous Caryatid porch, and the small Temple of Athena Nike — an ensemble UNESCO calls the greatest architectural and artistic complex bequeathed by Greek antiquity to the world (site 404, inscribed 1987). The rock has been inhabited since the Neolithic and served as fortress, sanctuary and symbol in turn; since 1975 a Greek state restoration programme has been re-setting its marble buildings block by block, in Pentelic marble and titanium.

## Visitor guide

**Best time.** Book the first or last slot of the day: the rock is exposed, the summer midday heat is the classic mistake, and the earliest entry beats the cruise-group wave. Seasonal hours are published on the official platform — in recent seasons the site has run roughly 08:00–20:00 in summer and 08:00–17:00 in winter, closing on 1 January, 25 March, 1 May, Easter Sunday and 25–26 December; treat the platform's current posting, not this file, as authoritative.

**How to reach and book.** Entry is by mandatory timed slot booked on **hhticket.gr**, the Greek Ministry of Culture's official e-ticket platform (Hellenic Heritage). Tickets are tied to a short entry window around the chosen time; daily visitor numbers are capped (about 20,000). The Acropolis Museum, at the foot of the rock, is a separate ticket and a separate visit — the original Caryatids and the Parthenon gallery are there. Prices change by season and are deliberately not printed here; the official platform carries the current figures.

## What to wear (framework — keyed to the live weather layer)

Read the live conditions block on the page first. The rock has no shade and reflects heat: in summer, a hat, sunscreen and water are equipment, not comforts, and marble worn smooth by millions of feet is slippery — shoes with grip, never smooth soles. In the winter band, the summit wind cuts: a windproof layer. In rain, treat every marble step as ice.

## Myth vs Fact

**Myth: The Parthenon is perfectly straight and regular. — FALSE.** Almost the opposite: columns swell and lean inward, the floor curves upward — the temple is a masterpiece of deliberate irregularity (entasis and curvature) that only *looks* perfectly straight. *(Correction: Wikipedia — "Acropolis of Athens", as recorded in the data row.)*

**Myth: The marble was always gleaming white. — FALSE.** Traces of pigment show the sculptures and architectural details were painted in colour; the white ruin is what 2,400 years left behind. *(Wikipedia — "Acropolis of Athens".)*

**Myth: The missing sculptures were destroyed in antiquity. — FALSE.** Most of the removed Parthenon sculptures survive — divided since the early 1800s between the Acropolis Museum in Athens and the British Museum in London, in an ongoing dispute between Greece and the UK. *(Wikipedia — "Acropolis of Athens".)*
