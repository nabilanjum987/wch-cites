## Overview

Pakistan is a South Asian federation of four provinces — Punjab, Sindh, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Balochistan — plus the Islamabad Capital Territory and the northern territories of Gilgit-Baltistan and Azad Jammu and Kashmir. The capital is Islamabad. The Pakistan Bureau of Statistics' 2023 census counted 241.49 million people across the four provinces and the capital territory; the World Bank's 2025 population estimate is 255.2 million. The currency is the Pakistani rupee (PKR); Urdu is the national language and English the official language of administration.

The country runs from the Karakoram — some of the highest mountains on earth — through the Indus plain to the Arabian Sea coast, and its cities in this bank span that whole range: the three live cities (Lahore, Karachi, Islamabad) and thirteen packaged cities from Quetta at 1,680 metres to Hyderabad at 13.

## Economy narrative

The World Bank measured Pakistan's GDP at US$407.3 billion in 2025, with GDP per capita of about US$1,596, inflation of 3.55% and unemployment of 5.42% (World Bank, 2025). The structure behind those numbers: agriculture on the Indus irrigation system — one of the largest in the world — a textile-led manufacturing sector centred on cities such as Faisalabad and Sialkot, and a services economy concentrated in Karachi (finance and the ports), Lahore and the twin cities of Rawalpindi–Islamabad.

## Provinces intro

Punjab is the largest province by population — 127.69 million at the 2023 census, more than half the country — and its agricultural and industrial engine (Lahore, Faisalabad, Gujranwala, Multan, Rawalpindi, Sialkot, Sargodha, Bahawalpur are all packaged in this bank). Sindh (55.70 million, 2023) holds Karachi, the country's largest city and port, plus Hyderabad and Sukkur. Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (40.86 million, 2023) is the mountainous north-west: Peshawar, Mardan, Abbottabad. Balochistan (14.89 million, 2023) is the largest by area and the least dense, with Quetta as its capital. The Islamabad Capital Territory counted 2.36 million in 2023.

## Practical & visitor prose

Pakistan's practical frame varies by altitude more than by distance: Quetta and Abbottabad have cold winters, Karachi a sea-moderated heat, and Multan and Sukkur some of the hottest summers in the country — the live weather layer on each city page is the working reference. Domestic travel is by road and rail between the plains cities, with air links joining the provincial capitals. Emergency numbers are national: police 15, ambulance (Rescue 1122) 1122, fire 16.
