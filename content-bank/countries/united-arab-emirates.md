## Overview

The United Arab Emirates is a federation of seven emirates on the Gulf's south-eastern shore — 83,600 km² (packaged) with a 2025 population of 11,513,149 (World Bank, packaged). The capital is Abu Dhabi; the largest city is Dubai. The currency is the UAE dirham (AED); the official language is Arabic, with English in universal daily use; the calling code is +971. Three of its cities — Abu Dhabi, Dubai and Sharjah — are packaged in this bank's B1 set.

## Economy narrative

The packaged World Bank snapshot: GDP US$552.3 billion (2024), growth 3.99%, GDP per capita US$50,274 — the federation pairs an energy economy centred on Abu Dhabi with the trade, tourism, logistics and finance economy centred on Dubai. Unemployment was 2.17% and inflation 1.25% (2025) in the packaged indicators, and life expectancy 83.1 years. The UAE is also one of the world's great remittance-sending countries — its large expatriate workforce sends earnings to Pakistan, India, Bangladesh and beyond, which is why the AED pairs anchor the remittance evergreen in this bank.

## Provinces intro

The seven emirates (packaged): Abu Dhabi — by far the largest, holding the capital and the oil wealth; Dubai, the commercial hub; Sharjah, the cultural emirate; and the northern emirates of Ajman, Fujairah (the only one on the Gulf of Oman coast), Ras Al Khaimah and Umm Al Quwain. Each emirate keeps its own ruler and character inside the federation.

## Practical & visitor prose

The practical frame is the Gulf climate and the federation's infrastructure: extreme summer heat, a winter season of outdoor life, and inter-emirate highways that make the three banked cities one continuous urban system.
