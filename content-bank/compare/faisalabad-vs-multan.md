## The essay

Punjab's two southern giants are a study in what a city is *for*. Faisalabad, at 3,203,846 people in the 2017 census, is 1.71 times Multan's 1,871,843 — and the size difference is the smaller part of the story. Faisalabad is a planned industrial city of the canal-colony era, laid out around its Clock Tower and Eight Bazaars, built to collect the region's cotton and then to manufacture it: the "Manchester of Pakistan", whose packaged products — cotton textiles and garments, hosiery — make it an engine of national exports.

Multan is older by millennia and built around the opposite pole: the shrine. Its skyline is the blue dome of Shah Rukn-e-Alam on the fort mound; its calendar is the urs; its packaged products are things made by hand and by orchard — Sohan Halwa, khussa footwear, blue pottery, chaunsa mangoes. Where Faisalabad sells cloth to the world, Multan sells craft, sweetness and sanctity to the country.

Even the famous names divide along the same line. Faisalabad's packaged figures are musicians — Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan and Rahat Fateh Ali Khan, qawwali's royal family — performers whose art travels. Multan's are saints — Bahauddin Zakariya and his grandson Shah Rukn-e-Alam — figures to whom the world travels, plus Inzamam-ul-Haq for the modern register. The foods agree: Faisalabad's chikar cholay and chargha are working-city food; Multan's Sohan Halwa and chaunsa are gifts, carried home in boxes.

The elevations (184 m against 122 m) make both of them plains cities of the same hot Punjab summer — Multan's heat is the more famous of the two — but the comparison the packages support is not climatic. It is functional: the loom against the shrine, the bazaar grid against the pilgrim's quarter, the country's textile engine against the City of Saints.

**Verdict: Faisalabad is Punjab's industrial giant — 1.7 times Multan's population and Pakistan's textile engine — while Multan, the older and smaller city, is its spiritual and craft capital; together they are the two halves of the south Punjab economy.**
