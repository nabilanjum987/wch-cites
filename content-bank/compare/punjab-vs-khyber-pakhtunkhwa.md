## The essay

Punjab against Khyber Pakhtunkhwa is the comparison between Pakistan's plain and its north-western mountains — and the census draws the contrast in one row above all: urban share. Punjab is 40.70% urban; KP is 15.01% urban — 84.99% rural, the most rural of Pakistan's large provinces. KP's population gathers in the Peshawar valley and a scatter of district towns (Peshawar, Mardan and Abbottabad are the KP cities banked in B1), while most of the province's 40.86 million people live in villages across the hills.

In people, Punjab outweighs KP 3.13 to one — 127.69 million against 40.86 million — and its share of the nation (52.87%) is more than three times KP's (16.92%). Yet the density row complicates the picture of an "empty" north-west: at about 402 people per km² across 101,741 km², KP is far from sparse — its valleys are crowded; it is the mountains between them that are empty. Punjab's 622 per km² is spread across a province twice KP's size.

Growth adds a quiet surprise: KP's 2.38% a year is the *slowest* of the four provinces in the 2023 census — slower than Punjab's 2.53% — despite its younger, rural profile, a pattern the census ties to out-migration toward Punjab's cities and abroad. The comparison therefore ends in a paradox the figures fully support: Pakistan's demographic centre of gravity sits in Punjab, and a measurable part of KP's story is written by the KP-born people who move there.

**Verdict: Punjab holds over half of Pakistan and outweighs KP three-to-one, urbanising at nearly three times KP's rate — while KP, 85% rural and the slowest-growing large province, remains the country's mountain heartland.**
