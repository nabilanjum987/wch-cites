## The essay

Two countries that were one economy in 1947 now sit at different scales of almost everything the World Bank measures — and this table, built on identical indicators so that no row compares unlike with unlike, shows exactly where the difference is scale and where it is substance.

Scale first. India's population of 1.464 billion is 5.7 times Pakistan's 255.2 million; its GDP of US$3.956 trillion is 9.7 times Pakistan's US$407.3 billion. The economy gap (9.7×) is much wider than the people gap (5.7×), and the quotient of the two is the comparison's human core: GDP per capita of US$2,702 against about US$1,596 — the average Indian income is roughly 1.7 times the average Pakistani income. That ratio, not the headline trillions, is what the matched rows actually say about daily life.

The price and jobs rows lean the same way, more gently: India's inflation of 2.40% against Pakistan's 3.55%, unemployment of 4.22% against 5.42% (both 2025). Then the table turns: life expectancy runs the other way — 73.5 years in Pakistan against 72.2 in India (2024) — the single row in the set where Pakistan leads, and a reminder that the comparison is not one-directional in everything that matters.

The remittance row is left deliberately one-sided, because that is how the verified packages have it: India's US$150.7 billion inflow (2025) is in its country package; Pakistan's corresponding figure was not in the matched row set, so this narrative does not quote one. What can be said from the set alone is structural: both economies are substantially carried by their diasporas, and India's inflow is the largest recorded in this bank's country packages.

The honest summary of the matched figures is not a contest result but a proportion: the same civilisational economy, divided in 1947, now operates at roughly ten-to-one in output, six-to-one in people, and five-to-three in income per head — with the health row quietly pointing the other way.

**Verdict: India outweighs Pakistan on every economic row of the matched set — about 9.7× the GDP and 1.7× the income per head — while Pakistan leads on the set's one health row, life expectancy; it is a comparison of scale, not of kind.**
