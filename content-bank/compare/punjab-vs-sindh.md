## The essay

This is the comparison between Pakistan's majority and its second province — and the first thing the figures say is how unequal the pairing is. Punjab's 127.69 million people are 52.87% of the country: one province containing more Pakistanis than the other three provinces and the capital combined. Sindh's 55.70 million is a large province by any world standard, yet it is only 2.29 times smaller than Punjab — the gap between first and second in Pakistan is a chasm, not a step.

Where Sindh answers is urbanisation. At 53.73% urban, Sindh is the only province of Pakistan where city-dwellers outnumber villagers — the gravitational effect of Karachi, the country's largest city and port. Punjab, at 40.70% urban, remains a province of countryside and market towns despite holding Lahore, Faisalabad, Rawalpindi and a string of million-plus industrial cities. The two provinces thus represent Pakistan's two futures at once: Punjab the demographic and agricultural weight, Sindh the urban and maritime form.

Growth refuses to separate them: 2.53% a year for Punjab against 2.57% for Sindh across the census period — Sindh growing a fraction faster from a much smaller base, which means the absolute arithmetic still widens Punjab's lead by millions per census cycle. Density completes the picture: about 622 people per km² in Punjab against about 395 in Sindh, the difference between the intensively farmed Indus plain of the north and a province whose west is Karachi's sprawl and whose north is riverine farmland, with arid country between.

Read together, the census describes a federation with a heavyweight and a city-state in one frame: Punjab decides the country's demography; Sindh decides what its urbanisation looks like.

**Verdict: Punjab outweighs Sindh two-to-one in people and holds over half the nation — but Sindh, majority-urban around Karachi, is the more urbanised province, and the two are growing at statistically the same pace.**
