## The essay

No pairing in Pakistan's census makes the country's extremes touch like Sindh against Balochistan. Begin with land: Balochistan's 347,190 km² is the largest province — roughly 44% of the country's territory — and two and a half times Sindh's 140,914 km². Now the people: Sindh's 55.70 million is nearly four times Balochistan's 14.89 million. Divide one by the other and the census produces its most dramatic number: about 395 people per km² in Sindh against about 43 per km² in Balochistan — a nine-fold density gap between two provinces that share a border.

Urbanisation sharpens it. Sindh, at 53.73% urban, is Pakistan's only majority-urban province, organised around Karachi's millions. Balochistan, at 30.96% urban, gathers what urban life it has into Quetta and a handful of towns across a territory the size of a European country; most Balochistanis live in settlements scattered through desert and mountain.

And then the growth row reverses the story's direction: Balochistan's 3.20% annual growth is the fastest of any province in the 2023 census — well ahead of Sindh's 2.57%. The emptiest province is filling fastest. The shares (23.06% against 6.17%) will not equalise in any foreseeable census, but the trajectory matters for everything from water to representation: Pakistan's map is increasingly drawn by a crowded, urbanising river province on one side and a vast, fast-growing, still-sparse highland on the other.

**Verdict: Sindh packs nearly four times Balochistan's people into two-fifths of its land at nine times the density — but Balochistan, the largest and sparsest province, is growing faster than any other, and the gap is slowly narrowing.**
