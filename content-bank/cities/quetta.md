## City intro

Quetta, the capital of Balochistan, counted 1,001,205 residents at the 2017 census. It is a high city — 1,680 metres — cupped in a valley ringed by mountains, and its nickname tells its economy: the Fruit Garden of Pakistan. Orchards of apples, grapes and almonds spread through the valley and the Urak road, and the city's cold-storage and commission markets move that fruit across the country.

Hanna Lake, in the hills above the city, and Hazarganji-Chiltan National Park, home of the Chiltan markhor, frame its outdoor life. The Quaid-e-Azam Residency at nearby Ziarat — where Pakistan's founder spent his last days — is the region's most visited historic site.

Quetta's own table is distinctive too: sajji, whole roasted lamb, and kaak, the stone-baked bread of Balochistan, washed down with Quetta kahwa.
