## City intro

Hyderabad, in Sindh, counted 1,732,693 residents at the 2017 census. It stands on the east bank of the Indus at just 13 metres elevation, and it is one of the cultural capitals of Sindh: the city of the Pacco Qillo fort, of glass bangle workshops, and of the Shahi Bazaar running below the fort walls.

Founded as a fort city of the Kalhora and Talpur rulers, Hyderabad preserves that layered history in the Sindh Museum, in Rani Bagh, and in its shrines and bazaars. Its craft identity is unusually specific: Hyderabad's glass bangles are worn across Pakistan, and its ajrak cloth and Sindhi caps are emblems of Sindhi culture.

The city is also an education centre for interior Sindh, associated with Allama I. I. Kazi, the founder of the University of Sindh, and his wife Elsa Kazi.
