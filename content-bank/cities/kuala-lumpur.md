## City intro

Kuala Lumpur is the capital of Malaysia — the packaged population figure of about 9,000,280 (2025, as packaged from Wikidata) is a metropolitan-scale count for the Klang Valley city region, and this file treats it as such rather than as a city-proper figure. Its symbol is the Petronas Twin Towers, for years the world's tallest buildings, and its layers run from Merdeka Square and the old colonial core to the KL Tower and the Perdana Botanical Gardens.

At the city's edge rise the Batu Caves, the limestone hill and temple complex that hosts Thaipusam. Kuala Lumpur's daily life is multilingual and multi-faith — Malay, Chinese, Indian and global communities sharing one street-food table.

## Economy

The packaged national frame (World Bank): Malaysia's GDP was US$472.2 billion in 2025, growing 5.17%, with GDP per capita of US$13,125, inflation of 1.38% and unemployment of 3.76%. Kuala Lumpur is the country's financial, commercial and administrative centre within that economy. The package's local sources are the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM) and the Malaysia open data portal (data.gov.my). No city-level GDP figure is claimed: the package carries none.

## Living & practical

Kuala Lumpur is tropical: heat, humidity and afternoon downpours year-round; the live weather layer on the city page carries current conditions. The currency is the Malaysian ringgit (MYR). The packaged food list is the national table at its best: nasi lemak, char kway teow, satay, roti canai and teh tarik.

Emergency number (packaged): 999 for all emergency services.
