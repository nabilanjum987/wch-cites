## City intro

Dubai is the largest city of the United Arab Emirates — about 3,944,751 people (2025, as packaged from Wikidata) in the Asia/Dubai time zone. It is the city of the Burj Khalifa, the tallest building in the world, of the Palm Jumeirah islands, and of the Dubai Mall — a skyline built within living memory on a creek-side trading port.

The older Dubai survives along the Creek: the dhow wharves, the gold and spice souks across the water in Deira, and Jumeirah Mosque, the city's most photographed house of worship. Between the Creek and the towers sits one of the world's great aviation and trading crossroads.

Dubai's population is overwhelmingly international, and its calendar — trade fairs, shopping festivals, winter beach season — runs on that global traffic.

## Economy

The packaged national frame (World Bank): the UAE's GDP was US$552.3 billion in 2024, with GDP per capita of US$50,274, unemployment of 2.17% and inflation of 1.25% (2025). Dubai is the federation's trade, tourism, logistics and finance hub — the commercial counterweight to Abu Dhabi's administrative capital. ## Living & practical

Dubai is a desert city: extreme summer heat makes the live weather layer on the city page the first check of any day; winter is the outdoor season. The currency is the UAE dirham (AED). The Metro and the Creek abras make the city unusually legible for visitors.

The packaged food list mixes Emirati and the city's global table: al harees, luqaimat, machboos, shawarma and balaleet.
