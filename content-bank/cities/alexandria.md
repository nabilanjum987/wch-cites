## City intro

Alexandria is Egypt's Mediterranean city — about 5,362,517 people (2023, as packaged from Wikidata) in the Africa/Cairo time zone. Founded by Alexander the Great, it was the ancient world's great port of learning, and its modern symbols deliberately answer that past: the Bibliotheca Alexandrina, the twenty-first-century library raised near the site of the ancient one, and the Citadel of Qaitbay standing where the Pharos lighthouse once stood.

The Catacombs of Kom El Shoqafa descend into the city's Greco-Egyptian underworld; Montaza Palace gardens mark its royal-era east; and the Stanley Bridge lights its corniche at night. Alexandria is Egypt's second city and its summer capital in spirit — the country's window on the Mediterranean.
