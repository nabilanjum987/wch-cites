## City intro

Abbottabad is a hill city in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, set at 1,256 metres in the Orash Valley. The 2017 census counted 207,989 residents, making it a mid-sized city by Pakistani standards — but its role is outsized: it is the home of the Pakistan Military Academy at Kakul, the country's principal officer-training institution, and the natural gateway to the Galiyat hill country of Nathia Gali and Ayubia.

The city was founded in the British era and named after Major James Abbott, its first deputy commissioner, whose name the city still carries. Its cooler mountain climate, pine-covered ridges such as Shimla Hill, and landmarks like the Ilyasi Masjid — known for the spring that flows through it — give it a character closer to a hill station than to the plains cities of Punjab.

Abbottabad is also a market town for its hinterland: Galiyat honey, walnuts and woollen goods from the surrounding hills are traded through its bazaars, and its schools, hospitals and cantonment serve a wide mountain region.

## Economy

No verified city-level GDP series exists for Abbottabad, so this section works from the packaged data and the national frame. Pakistan's economy was measured by the World Bank at US$407.3 billion in 2025, with GDP per capita of about US$1,596, inflation of 3.55% and unemployment of 5.42% (World Bank, 2025). Abbottabad's economy sits inside that frame but is shaped locally by three anchors: the Pakistan Military Academy and the cantonment, which anchor public-sector employment; education and health services for the Hazara region; and trade in hill produce — honey, walnuts and woollens from the Galiyat — moving through the city's bazaars.

Tourism is the growth layer: the city is the staging point for the Galiyat resorts, and visitor traffic supports its hotels, restaurants and transport trades, especially in the summer season when plains residents travel up to the hills.

## Living & practical

Abbottabad's elevation (1,256 m) gives it mild summers and cold winters by Pakistani standards — the live weather layer on the city page carries current conditions. The city lies on the Karakoram Highway corridor north of Islamabad, which is its main road link to the capital region and onward to the northern areas.

Local food culture is part of everyday life here: chapli kebab and chicken karahi are staples, seekh kebab is a bazaar standard, and trout from the hill streams near the city appears on local menus.

Emergency numbers for Abbottabad follow the national set used across Pakistan: police 15, ambulance (Rescue 1122) 1122, fire brigade 16.

Notable figures linked to the city in the package include its founder James Abbott, and Sardar Mehtab Ahmed Khan and Sardar Haider Zaman Khan, both senior political figures of the Hazara region.
