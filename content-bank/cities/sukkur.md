## City intro

Sukkur, in northern Sindh, counted 551,357 residents at the 2017 census. It is the Gate of Sindh — the city of the river crossing, where the Indus narrows between limestone bluffs and three generations of engineering span it: the Sukkur Barrage of 1932, one of the great irrigation works of the world; the Lansdowne Bridge; and the Ayub Bridge beside it.

The city stands at 67 metres on the west bank, facing Rohri across the water. Its skyline carries the Minaret of Masum Shah, and the island temple of Sadh Belo sits in the river — a sacred site reached by boat.

Sukkur's markets run on the river's produce: dates from the district's groves, palla fish from the Indus, and cotton from the barrage-irrigated plain.
