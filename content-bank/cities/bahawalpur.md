## City intro

Bahawalpur, in southern Punjab, is known as the City of Palaces. The 2017 census counted 682,116 residents. It was the capital of a princely state ruled by the Abbasi nawabs, and their buildings still define it: the Italianate Noor Mahal, the Darbar Mahal, and a royal necropolis tradition that reaches out to the Derawar Fort in the Cholistan desert nearby.

The city stands near the Sutlej river at an elevation of 118 metres, on the edge of the Cholistan. Lal Suhanra National Park, one of Pakistan's largest, lies within reach of the city, and the Bahawalpur Central Library is among the notable public buildings of the old state era.

Bahawalpur's identity mixes court heritage with desert-edge agriculture: dates and mangoes from its orchards, and Cholistan carpets, embroidery and pottery from its craft traditions.

Heritage tourism around the nawabi palaces and Derawar Fort is the city's distinctive service economy, drawing visitors into the Cholistan each winter season.

## Living & practical

Derawar Fort and Lal Suhanra are excursions rather than city sights: both are listed in the package as nearby landmarks, and both need a planned day trip.
