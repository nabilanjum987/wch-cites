## City intro

Cairo is the capital of Egypt — about 9,801,536 people in the city proper (2023, as packaged from Wikidata), in the Africa/Cairo time zone, with a metropolitan sprawl far larger. It is the city of a thousand minarets: the Citadel of Saladin above the old city, Al-Azhar Mosque and its university, the bazaar streets of Khan el-Khalili, and Coptic Cairo's churches in the south.

The Egyptian Museum's collections anchor its Pharaonic layer, while Giza's pyramids stand at the desert edge of the metropolis. Cairo is the Arab world's largest city and its media and education capital — a city where every era since the Pharaohs has left a standing layer.

## Economy

The packaged national frame (World Bank): Egypt's GDP was US$365.3 billion in 2025, with GDP per capita of US$3,086, inflation of 14.07%, unemployment of 6.78% and remittances of US$41.5 billion — remittances being one of the economy's great inflows, much of it worked by Egyptians in the Gulf. Cairo concentrates the state's government, universities and services within that economy. The package's local sources are CAPMAS publications. No city-level GDP figure is claimed: the package carries none.

## Living & practical

Cairo is a desert city: hot summers, mild winters, and dust as a seasonal fact; the live weather layer on the city page carries current conditions. The currency is the Egyptian pound (EGP). The packaged food list is the national table: koshari, ful medames, ta'ameya, mahshi and umm ali.
