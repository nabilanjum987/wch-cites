## City intro

Multan, in southern Punjab, is the City of Saints. The 2017 census counted 1,871,843 residents. It is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities of the region, and its skyline is defined by shrines: the great Tomb of Shah Rukn-e-Alam, with its blue-glazed dome on the old fort mound, and the Tomb of Bahauddin Zakariya nearby — two of the most important Sufi monuments in Pakistan.

The old city gathers around the fort hill (Qilla Kohna Qasim Bagh), the Ghanta Ghar clock tower and the Hussain Agahi Bazaar. Around those landmarks sit the trades the city is famous for: blue pottery, Multani khussa footwear, and Sohan Halwa, the sweet that carries the city's name across the country.

Multan is also mango country — its chaunsa mangoes are a national benchmark — and the heat that ripens them shapes the rhythm of the city year.

## Living & practical

Multan is one of Pakistan's hottest cities (122 m elevation); summers are extreme and the live weather layer on the city page is essential reading before a shrine visit. The shrine quarter and bazaars are dense and walkable in the cooler hours.

The packaged food list: Sohan Halwa, chaunsa mangoes in season, Multani falooda, and lassi.

Emergency numbers follow the national set: police 15, ambulance (Rescue 1122) 1122, fire brigade 16.

Notable figures in the package: the Sufi saints Bahauddin Zakariya and his grandson Shah Rukn-e-Alam, and cricketer Inzamam-ul-Haq.
