## City intro

Kuwait City is the capital of Kuwait — about 2,989,000 people (2018, as packaged from Wikidata; a metropolitan-scale figure for the capital region) in the Asia/Kuwait time zone. Its symbols stand along the Gulf shore: the Kuwait Towers with their blue-green spheres, the Liberation Tower, and the Grand Mosque of Kuwait, the country's largest.

The old city's heart is Souq Al-Mubarakiya, the market of spice stalls and cafés that predates the oil era, and Al Shaheed Park gives the dense city its green museum-ground. Kuwait City is the seat of one of the world's oldest oil economies and one of its strongest currencies.

## Economy

The packaged national frame (World Bank): Kuwait's GDP was US$157.2 billion in 2025, with GDP per capita of US$32,312, unemployment of 2.18%, inflation of 2.36% and life expectancy of 84.6 years — the highest in this country set. The Kuwaiti dinar (KWD) is the country's currency. The capital concentrates the state, oil and finance functions. The package's local sources are Kuwait Central Statistical Bureau publications and the Kuwait open data portal. No city-level GDP figure is claimed: the package carries none.

## Living & practical

Kuwait City is one of the hottest capitals on earth in summer; the live weather layer on the city page is essential planning input, and public life is an evening affair for months of the year. The packaged food list is the Gulf table with Kuwaiti signatures: machboos, harees, gers ogaily (the saffron-cardamom cake), balaleet and samak mashwi (grilled fish).
