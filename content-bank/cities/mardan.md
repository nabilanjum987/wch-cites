## City intro

Mardan, in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, counted 363,788 residents at the 2017 census. It sits in a fertile plain at 311 metres and is best understood as a gateway to one of the richest archaeological landscapes in Pakistan: the Gandhara Buddhist heritage of the Peshawar valley.

Two of that heritage's greatest sites stand nearby and are listed in the city's package: Takht-i-Bahi, the Buddhist monastery complex preserved on its ridge, and Shahbaz Garhi, where edicts of the emperor Ashoka survive carved in stone. In the city itself, the Guides Memorial recalls the Kabul embassy rescue of the colonial era, and the Mardan Museum holds Gandhara material.

Mardan is also a working agricultural city of the valley — sugar, tobacco and marble from the surrounding districts move through its markets, and its chapli kebab carries the city's name across the province.
