## City intro

Abu Dhabi is the capital of the United Arab Emirates — a city of about 1,570,000 people (2023, as packaged from Wikidata) spread along a Gulf island shore in the Asia/Dubai time zone. Where its neighbour Dubai built vertical, Abu Dhabi built grand: the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque, whose white domes anchor the city's image, the Emirates Palace hotel, and the historic Qasr al-Hosn, the oldest stone building in the city.

The cultural layer is deliberate: the Louvre Abu Dhabi on Saadiyat Island gives the capital a museum of global standing, and the Corniche Road runs the city's waterfront length, its promenade the everyday public space of the capital.

Abu Dhabi is the seat of the UAE's federal government and the centre of its oil wealth — the administrative and energy capital of the federation.

## Economy

The packaged national frame (World Bank): the UAE's GDP was US$552.3 billion in 2024, with GDP per capita of US$50,274, unemployment of 2.17%, inflation of 1.25% (2025) and life expectancy of 83.1 years. Abu Dhabi, as the emirate holding the federation's principal hydrocarbon reserves and its sovereign institutions, is the economic centre of gravity behind those figures; its packaged local sources are the UAE open data portal (Bayanat) and Statistics Centre Abu Dhabi (SCAD) publications. No city-level GDP figure is claimed: the package carries none, so neither does this file.

## Living & practical

Abu Dhabi is a Gulf city: summers are extremely hot and the live weather layer on the city page is the practical starting point for any day outdoors; the Corniche is an evening space for much of the year. The currency is the UAE dirham (AED), the language of administration Arabic with English in wide daily use.

The packaged food list is Emirati: al harees, thareed, machboos, luqaimat and khanfaroosh.

Emergency numbers (packaged): police 999, ambulance 998, fire 997.
