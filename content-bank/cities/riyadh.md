## City intro

Riyadh is the capital of Saudi Arabia — a city of about 7,009,100 people (2022, as packaged from Wikidata) in the Asia/Riyadh time zone, risen from a walled oasis town into a desert metropolis. Its landmark is the Kingdom Centre, the tower with the inverted arch that defines the skyline, and its counterweight is Diriyah, the original Saudi capital on the city's north-west edge, now its heritage showpiece.

The National Museum and the King Abdulaziz Historical Center tell the unification story; the Al Rajhi Grand Mosque anchors its religious life. Riyadh is the Kingdom's seat of government, finance and decision-making — the administrative centre of the Gulf's largest economy.

## Economy

The packaged national frame (World Bank): Saudi Arabia's GDP was US$1.277 trillion in 2025, growing 4.5%, with GDP per capita of US$34,537, unemployment of 3.04% and inflation of 2.04%. Riyadh concentrates the state and finance functions behind those figures, and its expansion is the centrepiece of the Kingdom's diversification programme. The package's local sources are the Saudi Open Data Portal (GASTAT) and official municipal publications. No city-level GDP figure is claimed: the package carries none.

## Living & practical

Riyadh is a desert capital: summers are extremely hot and dry, winters mild; the live weather layer on the city page carries current conditions. The currency is the Saudi riyal (SAR). The packaged food list is Najdi and national: kabsa, jareesh, saleeg, mutabbaq and qahwa (Arabic coffee).
