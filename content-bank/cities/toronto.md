## City intro

Toronto is Canada's largest city — about 2,794,356 people (2021 census figure, as packaged from Wikidata) on the north shore of Lake Ontario, in the America/Toronto time zone. Its marker is the CN Tower, for decades the world's tallest free-standing structure, and its texture is neighbourhoods: the St. Lawrence Market halls, Casa Loma on its hill, the Royal Ontario Museum, and the Toronto Islands a ferry ride from the towers.

Toronto is one of the most multicultural cities in the world — its diversity is not a slogan but its street-level reality, in its food, festivals and languages. It is not the capital — Ottawa is — but it is Canada's financial and cultural centre.

## Economy

The packaged national frame (World Bank): Canada's GDP was US$2.320 trillion in 2025, with GDP per capita of US$55,698, inflation of 2.07% and unemployment of 6.91%. Toronto is the country's banking and markets centre within that economy. The package's local sources are the Statistics Canada Web Data Service and the City of Toronto Open Data portal. No city-level GDP figure is claimed: the package carries none.

## Living & practical

Toronto winters are genuinely cold and summers warm; the live weather layer on the city page carries current conditions, and winter visitors should plan around them. The currency is the Canadian dollar (CAD). The packaged food list: the peameal bacon sandwich (a St. Lawrence Market signature), poutine, butter tart, the Beaver Tail pastry, and the Toronto street hot dog.

Emergency number (packaged): 911 for police, ambulance and fire.
