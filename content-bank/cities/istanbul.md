## City intro

Istanbul is Türkiye's largest city — about 15,655,924 people (2023, as packaged from Wikidata) — and the only great city in the world that stands on two continents, divided by the Bosphorus in the Europe/Istanbul time zone. It has been Byzantium, Constantinople and Istanbul, and every layer is still standing.

The historic peninsula holds the packaged landmark set: the Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque facing it across Sultanahmet Square, Topkapi Palace above the Golden Horn, and the Grand Bazaar's vaulted streets. Across the water, the Galata Tower marks the old Genoese quarter.

Few cities carry their history as daily infrastructure: ferries cross between continents on commuter schedules, and the bazaars still trade. Istanbul is not Türkiye's capital — Ankara is — but it is its economic and cultural centre of gravity.

## Economy

The packaged national frame (World Bank): Türkiye's GDP was US$1.597 trillion in 2025, with GDP per capita of US$18,599, unemployment of 8.52%, and inflation of 34.88% (2025) — the high-inflation context that shapes prices across the city. Istanbul is the country's commercial capital: finance, trade, tourism and manufacturing concentrate here. The package's local sources are TurkStat publications and Eurostat's candidate-country datasets. No city-level GDP figure is claimed: the package carries none.

## Living & practical

Istanbul has four true seasons; the live weather layer on the city page carries current conditions, and the Bosphorus wind is a year-round factor. The currency is the Turkish lira (TRY). The packaged food list is the city's street canon: kebab, simit, balık ekmek (the fish sandwich of the Galata bridge), Turkish delight and baklava.

Emergency number (packaged): 112 for police, ambulance and fire.
