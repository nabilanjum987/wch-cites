## City intro

Jeddah is Saudi Arabia's Red Sea gateway — a city of about 4,697,000 people (2021, as packaged from Wikidata) in the Asia/Riyadh time zone. For centuries it has been the port of Makkah: pilgrims arriving by sea entered the Hijaz through Jeddah, and the old town of Al-Balad, with its coral-stone tower houses and wooden roshan balconies, still carries that history.

The modern city runs north along the Corniche: King Fahd's Fountain jetting from the sea, the floating Al-Rahmah Mosque at the shore, and Jeddah Tower rising as the city's statement of ambition.

Jeddah's temperament is the Kingdom's most open: a merchant city, a pilgrim gateway and a seaside resort in one.

## Economy

The packaged national frame (World Bank): Saudi Arabia's GDP was US$1.277 trillion in 2025, growing 4.5%, with GDP per capita of US$34,537, unemployment of 3.04% and inflation of 2.04%. Jeddah's role inside that economy is the western gateway: the Kingdom's principal Red Sea port city and the arrival point for pilgrimage traffic to Makkah. ## Living & practical

Jeddah is hot and humid on the Red Sea coast; the live weather layer on the city page carries current conditions, and the Corniche is an evening city. The currency is the Saudi riyal (SAR). The packaged food list is Hijazi: saleeg, mutabbaq, sayadieh (the fisherman's fish and rice), foul and masoub.
