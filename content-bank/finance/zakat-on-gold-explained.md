# Zakat on Gold — Explained

*Evergreen explainer for the rates hub, where gold prices are quoted daily. This article explains the mainstream scholarly framework — the nisab, the rate, the points where schools differ. **It is an explanation, not a ruling (fatwa).** Individual circumstances differ; for a binding answer on your own case, consult a qualified scholar or your local dar al-ifta.*

## The obligation in outline

Zakat is the obligatory annual charity on qualifying wealth — one of the five pillars of Islam. Gold and silver are among its original subjects: the Qur'an warns those who hoard gold and silver without spending in God's way (9:34–35), and the Prophet's practice fixed the thresholds and the rate that the schools of law have applied since. Recipients are the eight categories of Qur'an 9:60, headed by the poor and the needy.

## The nisab — the threshold that triggers the duty

Zakat is due only when a person's zakatable wealth reaches the **nisab** — a minimum measured in gold or silver weight — and stays at or above it for a full lunar year (the *hawl*). The classical measures are **20 mithqal (dinar) of gold** and **200 dirhams of silver**. Converted to modern weights, two conventions are in use:

- **87.48 grams of gold / 612.36 grams of silver** — the conversion standard in Pakistan and much of South Asia (7.5 tola of gold; 52.5 tola of silver), used by the subcontinent's fatwa bodies.
- **85 grams of gold / 595 grams of silver** — the conversion used by many Arab fatwa bodies and international standards.

Both descend from the same classical measures with slightly different gram equivalents for the mithqal and dirham. This site serves a Pakistani audience first, so its examples use the **87.48 g / 612.36 g** standard — the difference is noted wherever the number matters, and either way, the nisab is a *weight*: its money value moves with the gold and silver prices on the live rates pages, which is why the calculation must be done at current prices.

**Which nisab for money?** Cash, bank balances and trade goods have no weight of their own, so they are valued against a nisab. The widely applied practice — and the standard ruling in Pakistan — is to measure money against the **silver nisab**, because it is the lower threshold: it obliges more people and delivers more to the poor, which scholars who recommend it describe as the more cautious course for the giver and the better one for the recipient. Some scholars permit using the gold nisab; the difference is one of the points to settle with your own scholar.

## The rate and the calculation

The rate is **2.5% — one-fortieth** — of the zakatable wealth above the nisab, once the hawl has passed. In practice, for gold:

1. **Weigh the gold and find its pure content.** Zakat reckons pure gold: 22-karat jewellery is 22⁄24 pure (91.6%), so 100 g of 22K contains about 91.6 g of pure gold. Karat conversion is arithmetic, not opinion.
2. **Value it at the current price** — the day's rate for that purity (the sarafa board's rates, explained in "How Pakistan gold pricing works" in this bank, are the natural reference in Pakistan).
3. **Add other zakatable assets** (cash, silver, trade goods, receivables), **deduct debts due**, and check the total against the nisab.
4. **Pay 2.5%** of the total zakatable amount — in cash value, or in kind.

*Worked illustration (round numbers, not rates):* 10 tola of 22K gold valued at an illustrative Rs 440,000 per tola is Rs 4,400,000 of gold; with Rs 100,000 cash the zakatable total is Rs 4,500,000 — well above the silver nisab (52.5 tola of silver at an illustrative Rs 5,000 per tola = Rs 262,500) — so zakat is 2.5% × 4,500,000 = **Rs 112,500**. The method is the content here; the prices in any real calculation must be the day's own.

## Where the schools differ — jewellery

The best-known difference of opinion concerns **gold jewellery in personal use**:

- **Hanafi school** (the majority tradition in Pakistan): zakat is due on gold and silver **whether worn, stored or invested** — jewellery in regular use is included.
- **Maliki, Shafi'i and Hanbali majority view:** jewellery prepared for **permissible personal use**, within customary limits, is **exempt**; gold held as investment, hoard or trade stock is zakatable by agreement.

There is no disagreement that bullion, coins and investment gold are zakatable, nor on the 2.5% rate, nor on the hawl. Because this article's readers will mostly follow one tradition or the other, it presents both positions as the mainstream scholarship states them and rules on neither.

## A closing word on method

Gold ownership is emotional as well as financial — bridal sets, inheritances, a mother's bangles. The framework above is deliberately mechanical about weights, purity and prices, because that is the part a rates website can honestly help with: today's per-tola price, the karat arithmetic, the threshold in rupees at today's silver rate. The part it cannot do — applying the law to your family's exact holdings — belongs to a scholar who can ask you questions. Use the live gold and silver pages for the numbers; use a mufti for the ruling.
