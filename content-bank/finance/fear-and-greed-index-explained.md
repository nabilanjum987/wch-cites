# The Fear & Greed Index — Explained

*Evergreen explainer for the rates hub, where a Fear & Greed reading appears in the live ticker. There are two famous indices with this name, and confusing them produces nonsense — this article keeps them separate and explains both, from their publishers' own methodologies.*

## The idea

Markets are moved by emotion as well as arithmetic, and the Fear & Greed idea is to compress that emotion into one number between **0 (extreme fear)** and **100 (extreme greed)**, with 50 as neutral. A very fearful market is one where investors are dumping risk at any price; a very greedy one is where they are chasing it at any price. The index is a thermometer, not a forecast: it describes the mood that produced recent prices, and traders watch it partly as a contrarian signal — extreme readings have historically clustered near turning points, without timing them.

## The original: CNN's stock-market index

CNN Business publishes the best-known version, for the US stock market. Its methodology, in the publisher's own description: seven indicators, each measured by **how far it has veered from its own average, relative to how far it normally veers**, each scored 0–100, and then combined **equally weighted** into the final reading. The seven are:

1. **Stock price momentum** — the S&P 500 against its 125-day moving average.
2. **Stock price strength** — NYSE stocks making 52-week highs versus 52-week lows.
3. **Stock price breadth** — volume in advancing versus declining shares.
4. **Put and call options** — the put/call ratio (more puts = more fear).
5. **Junk bond demand** — the yield spread between junk and investment-grade bonds (a narrowing spread = appetite for risk = greed).
6. **Market volatility** — the VIX.
7. **Safe haven demand** — stock returns versus Treasury bond returns over 20 trading days (bonds winning = fear).

CNN's bands: 0–24 extreme fear, 25–44 fear, 45–55 neutral, 56–74 greed, 75–100 extreme greed.

## The crypto version: Alternative.me

The index most live tickers actually display — including crypto dashboards — is **Alternative.me's Crypto Fear & Greed Index**, built for Bitcoin and published daily since 2018. The name is borrowed; the recipe is entirely different. Per the publisher's methodology, it blends:

- **Volatility — 25%:** current Bitcoin volatility and drawdowns against their 30- and 90-day averages (unusual spikes read as fear).
- **Market momentum / volume — 25%:** current volume and momentum against the same averages (heavy buying into a rising market reads as greed).
- **Social media — 15%:** interaction rates around Bitcoin topics.
- **Surveys — 15%:** listed in the methodology and currently paused by the publisher.
- **Dominance — 10%:** Bitcoin's share of total crypto market value (rising dominance reads as fear — money retreating to the safest crypto asset).
- **Trends — 10%:** Google search behaviour for Bitcoin-related terms.

Its bands are close to CNN's but not identical: 0–24 extreme fear, 25–44 fear, 45–55 neutral, 56–75 greed, 76–100 extreme greed.

## How to read a reading

Three rules keep the number honest. **Know which index you are looking at** — a crypto reading of 70 and a stock reading of 70 describe different markets and different recipes, and the two publishers' scores on the same morning can differ by twenty points without either being wrong. **Read the trend, not the tick** — the index is most informative as a series: weeks pinned in extreme fear describe a market pricing in disaster; a swing from fear to greed describes relief, not necessarily value. **Never trade on it alone** — it is a sentiment summary built from past prices and activity; it contains no information about what an asset is worth, and both publishers' presentations include the same caution in substance.

On this site, the ticker's Fear & Greed figure is the live daily reading from the crypto index's publisher feed; when it moves, the components above are where the movement came from.
