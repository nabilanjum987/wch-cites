# How Pakistan Gold Pricing Works — the Sarafa Board Mechanism

*Evergreen explainer for the rates hub. The mechanism below was verified against public sources on 2026-10-22 (see Sources). It explains how the per-tola rate is built; it deliberately prints no standing rate, because the rate is a daily number, not an evergreen fact.*

## The short answer

The gold rate quoted by a jeweller in Karachi, Lahore or Peshawar was not decided in that shop, or even in that city. Every morning, the **All Pakistan Sarafa Gems and Jewellers Association (APGJSA)** — the trade body of the sarafa (bullion and jewellery) markets — announces the rate for the day, built from the international gold price and the rupee's interbank exchange rate, and sarafa markets across the country open on that announcement. That is why the number on the news ticker is (small local differences aside) the same number everywhere in Pakistan.

## The chain, step by step

**1. The international price.** Gold trades worldwide in US dollars per troy ounce (the London bullion market and COMEX are the reference venues). That dollar price is the foundation — Pakistan imports essentially its monetary gold, so the local price begins abroad.

**2. The currency conversion.** The ounce price is converted to rupees at the **interbank USD/PKR parity** — the association's published rate sheet states that its prices use interbank exchange rates. This is the subtle step most buyers miss: the per-tola rate can rise on a morning when gold did nothing in dollars, purely because the rupee weakened overnight. Part of any "gold jump" in the headlines is often a currency move in costume.

**3. The tola conversion.** Pakistan prices gold by the tola, a unit of **11.6638 grams**, against the troy ounce of 31.1035 grams. The arithmetic is fixed:

> per-tola price (before premium) = international price per ounce × interbank USD/PKR × 11.6638 ÷ 31.1035

**4. The local premium.** On top of the converted parity the market adds a premium covering import and dealing costs and local supply-demand. The trade's published formula discussions put this at a few dollars per tola equivalent; the Karachi market's premium is the one most often quoted in the financial press. The premium is why the board rate is never *exactly* the converted spot price.

**5. The quotation.** The association quotes the benchmark for **24-karat gold (999 purity)**, per tola — with the derived rates for 10 grams and per gram published alongside. Boards quote in round figures. Lower karats are derived arithmetically by purity: 22K at 22⁄24 (×0.9167) of the 24K rate, 21K at ×0.875, 18K at ×0.75. Silver per tola is announced on the same sheet, from the international silver price by the same method.

## From board rate to the jeweller's counter

The board rate is the price of the *metal*. A finished piece adds:

- **Making charges** — the jeweller's labour, quoted per tola or as a percentage, negotiated shop by shop.
- **Wastage allowance** — traditionally charged on jewellery work.
- **Stones and embellishments** — priced separately, and usually excluded when the piece is sold back.

And in the other direction: jewellers typically **buy back below the board rate**, deducting for purity testing, melting loss and their margin. The board rate is therefore best understood as the day's reference price for pure gold — the number from which both the showroom price and the buy-back price are negotiated.

## Why the mechanism matters to a reader of this site

Because the formula has two moving parts — the dollar gold price and the rupee — the sarafa rate is really a product of *both* markets. This site's ticker shows them side by side for exactly that reason: gold per tola, USD/PKR interbank, and the international ounce, updating independently. When the tola figure moves, the honest first question is: which input moved?
