# Sending Money Home — a Remittance Guide for the Diaspora Pairs (USD / SAR / AED / GBP → PKR)

*Evergreen guide for the rates and news hubs. It explains the process and the concepts for the four corridors this site serves. It quotes no standing exchange rates — rates are live numbers and belong to the live layer. One dated, labelled illustration is included.*

## The four corridors

Millions of Pakistanis work abroad, concentrated in the Gulf and the United Kingdom, and the money they send home travels four main corridors into the rupee: **US dollars (USD→PKR)** from North America and worldwide dollar earners, **Saudi riyals (SAR→PKR)** — the largest Gulf workforce corridor, **UAE dirhams (AED→PKR)**, and **pounds sterling (GBP→PKR)** from Britain's Pakistani diaspora. The mechanics below are the same on all four; only the sending-side institutions differ.

## How a transfer actually travels

1. **The sender starts the transfer** with a bank, an exchange house (the Gulf's remittance houses are the classic channel), or a transfer app, paying in the local currency — riyals in Riyadh, dirhams in Dubai, pounds in London, dollars in New York.
2. **The provider converts and routes** the money. Formal transfers move through banking channels into Pakistan's banking system; the conversion to rupees is applied at the provider's or the receiving bank's rate for that day, which is anchored to the interbank market (see "Interbank vs open market rates" in this bank).
3. **The receiver in Pakistan** gets the money in one of three ways: **credited to a bank account** (an IBAN is all the sender needs), **credited to a mobile wallet**, or **paid out as cash** at a bank branch or agent, against the receiver's CNIC and the transfer reference number.

Since 2009 the **Pakistan Remittance Initiative (PRI)** — a joint framework of the State Bank of Pakistan, the Ministry of Finance and the Ministry of Overseas Pakistanis — has worked to keep these flows in the formal channels: faster settlement, wider agent networks, and incentive schemes that have at times made bank-channel sending free for the sender. The receiving side is free by design: money arriving through banking channels is credited in full, without tax deduction at receipt, under the framework that has applied to workers' remittances for decades.

## The only comparison that matters: rupees received

Every provider advertises something different — a headline rate, a "zero fee" banner, a first-transfer bonus. All of it collapses into one number: **how many rupees land, per unit sent, after everything**. A provider charging no fee but converting two rupees below the day's rate costs more on a £500 transfer than a provider charging £3 and converting at the day's rate. So:

- Compare the **total PKR delivered**, not the rate alone and not the fee alone.
- Check **which day's rate** a quote uses and whether it is locked at booking or set at delivery.
- For cash pickup, confirm the **payout network near your family** — a perfect rate is useless if collection costs a day of travel.
- Keep the **reference number and receipt**: formal channels give you a dispute path; that path is the product.

## What to avoid

The informal alternative — hundi/hawala through an unlicensed operator — is illegal under Pakistan's Foreign Exchange Regulation Act, leaves no enforceable record, and exposes both ends to seizure and prosecution risk. It survives by offering a rate a fraction better than the banks'; the fraction is the price of having no receipt, no recourse and no protection. Every corridor in this guide exists precisely so that nobody needs it.

## Practical checklist

- Sender: government ID as required in the sending country; the receiver's full name exactly as on their CNIC; IBAN for account credit.
- Receiver: CNIC for cash pickup; the transfer reference; collect within the payout window the provider states.
- Both: use licensed providers only; keep records; for regular support, a standing arrangement with one bank or exchange house builds a history that makes every later transfer easier.
- Overseas Pakistanis can also hold a **Roshan Digital Account** with a Pakistani bank (a State Bank of Pakistan initiative) for banking and investment from abroad — the official route for those who want a Pakistan account without travelling.
