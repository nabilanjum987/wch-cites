## Narration script
Welcome to the Rates hub of WorldCityHub — the page where this site keeps its money honest.

Rates are the most dangerous numbers on any website. They change constantly, they differ by source, and people make real decisions with them: the family converting remittances, the jeweller pricing a tola, the student paying fees abroad. So this page is built on a simple rule: every rate is either live from a named source, or it does not appear.

Start with gold, because in Pakistan gold is not an abstraction — it is savings, weddings, and security, priced by the tola. The page tracks spot gold through a traded bullion instrument and converts it honestly: on the twenty-second of October 2026, gold stood at four thousand and ninety dollars an ounce, which made one tola worth 424,557 rupees. Silver stands beside it, priced from a live spot feed — fifty-six dollars an ounce on the same morning. Below them, the currency board: the dollar at 277 rupees, the Saudi riyal at 73.87, the Emirates dirham at 75.43, the pound at 370.98 — the four conversions our audience asks for daily, because they are the currencies of the Gulf and British diasporas that send money home.

This page also carries a scar, and we show it deliberately. For years the site drew its exchange rates from a respected European reference service. That service moved hosts — and quietly stopped publishing the rupee, the riyal, and the dirham altogether. A careless site would have kept displaying the old page while its numbers froze. Ours caught the gap, recorded it, and rebuilt the feed on an open endpoint that still publishes the currencies our readers need. The lesson is printed in the page's own discipline: a rate without a living source behind it is worse than no rate at all.

Around the board sit the instruments of the wider market: Brent crude from the United States Federal Reserve's daily series — the latest close, labelled as such, never dressed as a live tick; Bitcoin from live exchange data; and the Fear and Greed index, which tells you what the market is feeling on a scale every investor recognises. Each has a section, an anchor, and a source.

Use the page as it is built to be used: glance at the board, then open the section you need — conversions into your own currency, the gold price in the unit you actually buy, the context for the day's move. And check the date on anything you rely on; where a figure is a daily close rather than a live price, the page says so in plain words.

Every number you have heard is dated the twenty-second of October 2026, and every one of them is refreshed on the page below — live, sourced, and waiting.

Two habits will make this page pay you back. The first is comparison across days: because each section keeps its dated readings honest, you can watch a trend form instead of trusting a stranger's chart. The second is suspicion — of any rate, anywhere, that arrives without a source and a timestamp. This page survives that suspicion: its gold can be traced to a live bullion market, its currencies to a named endpoint, its oil to a federal series, its sentiment index to its publisher. Bring the same questions here that you would bring to a money-changer's counter, and the page will answer every one of them. And one boundary, stated plainly: this page informs; it does not advise. It will show you the price of gold in the unit you buy it, the rate your remittance will meet, and the mood of the market — and then it stops, because the decision with your money is yours, and any page that pushes past information into instruction has forgotten whose money it is.
