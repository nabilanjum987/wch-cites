## Narration script
This is the Colosseum in Rome — the largest amphitheatre the ancient world ever built, and, nearly two thousand years after its first games, still the largest standing amphitheatre on Earth.

Our page starts with the documented skeleton. The Colosseum is an elliptical amphitheatre in the centre of Rome, just east of the Roman Forum. Construction began under the emperor Vespasian in the year 72 and was completed in AD 80 under his successor Titus, with further work under Domitian — the three emperors of the Flavian dynasty, which is why scholars also call it the Flavian Amphitheatre. It rose on the drained lake of Nero's golden palace, a deliberate political act: private luxury returned to the Roman people as public spectacle. The page's fact grid carries its status precisely too — the Colosseum stands within UNESCO's Historic Centre of Rome inscription of 1980.

What happened inside is the part everyone thinks they know, and the page's story sections separate the record from the cinema. Gladiatorial contests, wild-beast hunts, executions staged as theatre, and — in the early years, on the evidence of ancient writers — possibly spectacles involving water in the arena basin: each claim is carried at the strength of its evidence, because on this site a famous story earns a label, not a free pass. The building's later lives get equal honesty: fortress, quarry, Christian shrine, romantic ruin, and finally protected monument — the stone Rome rebuilt itself from came, in part, out of this shell, and the page does not hide the centuries when the Colosseum was a mine with a view.
