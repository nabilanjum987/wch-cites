## Narration script
This is the Burj Khalifa in Dubai — the tallest structure human beings have ever raised, and the youngest wonder in this series by roughly four and a half thousand years.

The verified figures first, because with this building the figures are the story. The Burj Khalifa stands 829.8 metres in total height, with a roof at 828 metres — designed by the Chicago firm Skidmore, Owings and Merrill, and the tallest building in the world since its inauguration, taking the title from Taipei 101. Its plan is a three-lobed spiral drawn, its architects say, from the desert flower and from the spiral minarets of the region: the tower steps back as it climbs, so the wind is confused out of its rhythm and the structure stands calm in a sky that should be tearing at it. Our page's fact grid records all of this with sources — and records one absence with equal care: unlike most of our wonders, the Burj Khalifa carries no UNESCO inscription, and the page says so plainly rather than letting the golden template's heritage box imply one.
