## Facts used
- Art Deco statue of Jesus in Rio de Janeiro; created by French-Polish sculptor Paul Landowski, built by Brazilian engineer Heitor da Silva Costa with French engineer Albert Caquot; the face by Gheorghe Leonida (Wikipedia).
- Constructed 1922–1931; 30 m high excluding the 8 m pedestal; arms stretch 28 m; reinforced concrete and soapstone; faces east.
- Stands on Corcovado mountain above Rio; the Rio landscape is UNESCO-listed (2012).

## Narration script
This is Christ the Redeemer — arms open over Rio de Janeiro, the most recognisable silhouette in South America, and a statue whose page on this site begins, as every golden page begins, with what can be proven about it.

The record, verified. The statue is an Art Deco figure of Jesus, created by the French-Polish sculptor Paul Landowski and built by the Brazilian engineer Heitor da Silva Costa, working with the French engineer Albert Caquot; the face was sculpted by Gheorghe Leonida. Construction ran from 1922 to 1931. The figure stands thirty metres tall, on an eight-metre pedestal, and its outstretched arms span twenty-eight metres. It is built of reinforced concrete and clothed in soapstone mosaic — millions of small stone tiles, applied by hand, which is why the statue glows rather than shines — and it faces east, over the harbour entrance, from its mountain above the city. That mountain is Corcovado, and the landscape around it — the bay, the peaks, the meeting of city and forest — is protected within UNESCO's listing of Rio's Carioca landscape, inscribed in 2012; the page states that status precisely, because the listing belongs to the landscape, and precision about honours is part of this site's religion.

The design itself has a story the page tells in its timeline: the first approved plan showed Christ holding a globe and a cross, and the organisers changed it to the open-armed figure the world knows — a design decision, documented, that turned a monument into an embrace. The photographs in the timeline carry their makers' credits, as always here.
