## Narration script
This is the Pacific Ocean — the largest thing on the surface of the Earth, an ocean so wide that all the planet's land could fit inside it with room to spare, and this video follows the page we built to measure it honestly.

Scale first, from the sources the page cites. The Pacific is the largest and deepest of the world's five oceanic divisions. It stretches from the Arctic Ocean in the north to the Southern Ocean — or Antarctica itself, depending on the definition in use, and the page notes that definitional seam rather than papering over it — bounded by Asia and Australia to the west and the Americas to the east. In its western depths runs the Mariana Trench, the deepest cut in the planet's crust, where the seafloor falls nearly eleven kilometres below the waves. An ocean that contains the world's deepest point and its largest surface is not a backdrop; it is the main geographical fact of the Earth, and the page treats it with that rank.

The golden ocean template then does what it did for the Arabian Sea, at continental scale. Identity is verified against the knowledge bases by description, not by name. The fact grid gives the ocean's bounds, its marginal seas, its island nations, and the figures surveys support — each with a source, each dated at the check. Where sources disagree on an area or a depth, the disagreement is printed, not resolved in secret; the Arabian Sea page set that precedent with its two depths, and the Pacific's page keeps it.

The live layer is chosen with the same restraint. No single buoy speaks for an ocean this size, so the page reads representative points — as its sister page does at sixteen north, sixty-four east — and labels them representative, with live wave and weather data from the marine feeds, and coastal conditions for the WorldCityHub cities that face this water. The pattern of the great gyres, the El Niño cycle that rearranges weather on three continents: explained as climatology, in its own labelled frame, never blended with the day's readings.

Why does a website of cities keep an ocean page at all? Because half the world's great ports wake up on this water, and because the template that learned honesty on a single sea should prove itself on the greatest one. The page below carries the facts, the sources, the live points, and the disagreements in the open — the whole standard, at Pacific scale.

The page's closing sections bring the ocean down to the shore, where our readers live. Its coasts carry more of humanity than any other ocean's rim — the ports of East and Southeast Asia, the Pacific face of the Americas, the island nations for whom this ocean is not a view but an address, a road, and a larder. For each WorldCityHub city that publishes on its shores, the ocean page and the city page point at each other: the city's weather reads the ocean's mood, and the ocean's live points explain the city's sky. That cross-reading is the site's whole idea in miniature — every subject documented once, honestly, and then allowed to illuminate its neighbours. The greatest ocean deserves nothing less, and neither do the cities that wake beside it.
