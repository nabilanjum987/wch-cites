## Narration script
This is the Eiffel Tower — the most recognised silhouette on Earth, and, for the purposes of this page and this video, something better than an icon: a documented structure, with dates, builders, and a live sky above it.

The facts, as our page lays them out. The tower stands on the Champ de Mars in Paris, a lattice of puddled iron designed and built between 1887 and 1889 by the company of Gustave Eiffel, whose name it carries. It rose as the entrance arch of the 1889 Exposition Universelle, the world's fair that marked the centenary of the French Revolution — a temporary showpiece, licensed for twenty years, that Paris simply never took down. The artists of the day protested it in print as an eyesore; within a generation it had become the city's signature, and then the planet's shorthand for an entire capital.

The golden template asks more of a page than admiration, so ours adds the layers a visitor — or a curious mind — actually needs. A fact grid with the tower's identity verified against Wikidata and the encyclopaedia, and its heritage status stated precisely: the tower is protected as part of UNESCO's "Paris, Banks of the Seine" inscription of 1991. A live layer for the Champ de Mars itself: Paris weather now, the sun's path across the tower's sky, and the golden hour when the iron turns the colour in the photographs. A timeline in images, from construction photographs of the rising lattice to the tower today. And the practical guide — official access information, dated, from official sources, because ticket rules change and this site dates its facts rather than disguising their age.
