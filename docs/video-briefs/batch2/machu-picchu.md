## Narration script
High in the Peruvian Andes, on a ridge between two peaks, sits Machu Picchu — a fifteenth-century Inca citadel that was never found by the empire that destroyed its builders, and this video follows the page we built to document it.

The verified ground first. Machu Picchu stands in the Eastern Cordillera of southern Peru, on a mountain ridge at 2,430 metres above the sea, some eighty kilometres northwest of Cusco, above the Sacred Valley where the Urubamba River bends around the mountain far below. It is a royal Inca estate of the fifteenth century — temples, terraces, storehouses, and houses fitted together in the Inca manner: stones cut so precisely to one another that the walls have ridden out five centuries of earthquakes. UNESCO inscribed it in 1983 as a mixed site, honouring both the building and the cloud-forest mountain that holds it. Each of those facts is on the page with its source; the estimates and open questions are labelled as what they are.

For Machu Picchu is a place where honest labelling matters more than most. The Spanish never reached it — that much the record supports — and it was never truly lost to the local farmers who knew the ridge all along, whatever the later explorer legends claim; the page's Myth versus Fact section handles the "lost city" story and the 1911 expedition that brought the site to the world's attention with the care both deserve, crediting the documented and questioning the dramatic. What the citadel was for — royal estate, ceremonial centre, astronomical instrument — scholarship still debates, and the page presents the debate rather than anointing a winner.

The visitor guide follows the official Peruvian sources and dates them: the circuits, the entry rules that protect the stones from the crowds that love them, the town below at Aguas Calientes, and the measured distances — the page measures; it does not round for romance.
