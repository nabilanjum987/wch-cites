## Narration script
Welcome to the Countries hub of WorldCityHub — the page where the whole world is laid out in one table, and where this site makes its biggest promise and keeps it carefully.

The heart of the page is the World Capitals table: one hundred and ninety-eight capitals, from Kabul to Zagreb, each with its country, its local time right now, and its current temperature. The times are computed from each capital's real time zone — not estimated, not copied from a static chart. The temperatures come from a single weather request covering every capital at once, refreshed on a half-hour rhythm, so when you compare a winter morning in Ottawa with a summer afternoon in Canberra, you are comparing readings from the same moment.

Why one hundred and ninety-eight, when our underlying dataset holds two hundred and forty-one rows? Because the rest are dependent territories — real places, worth their own guides one day, but not sovereign capitals, and the table does not pretend otherwise. That small act of honesty is the page in miniature.

Above the table sits the countries index itself, and here the discipline is stricter still. Today, exactly one country on this site is fully alive: Pakistan — its provinces, its published cities, its economy, its rates, its people, all rendered from real data. Other countries appear as what they honestly are: entries in a growing index, with their capitals in the table, waiting their turn behind the quality gate. This site activates a country only when its pages can render fully, with verified data, because a directory that lies about its coverage teaches visitors to trust nothing in it — including the parts that are true.

The order of growth is not random. Countries are being built the way the site was: Pakistan first as the template proof, then the countries its audience asks about most, each one required to pass the same gate Pakistan passed. When a country's card in this hub lights up with live figures, it will mean the same thing Pakistan's does: the data is real, the sources are named, and the page can be checked.

So use this hub two ways. As a traveller's instrument — what time is it in the capital, how cold, how far ahead or behind you — it answers immediately. And as a map of a building project, it shows you the honest state of the work: what is finished, what is verified, and what is still to come.

Everything in this video — the one hundred and ninety-eight capitals, the live times, the temperatures, and the one fully living country — is on the page below.

A note on how to read the table like a traveller. The time column is the one to linger on: because the times are computed, not printed, the table quietly teaches the shape of the turning Earth — which capitals are already in tomorrow while you read, which are still in yesterday, and how the day's first light travels from the Pacific capitals westward to your own. Pair it with the temperatures and the abstract map becomes a lived one: the cold capitals and the hot ones, the mornings and the midnights, all true at the same instant. That simultaneity is the page's quiet pleasure, and it costs you nothing but a scroll. Underneath the whole hub sits the country template itself: provinces and states, capitals, economies drawn through the one-cached-dataset rule this site learned the hard way, and personalities pooled from each country's cities. When the next country lights up, it will not be a summary page — it will be that full template, filled. The hub is where you will see it happen first.
