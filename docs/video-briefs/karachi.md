## Narration script
Welcome to Karachi — Pakistan's largest city, its financial engine, and its window on the Arabian Sea.

Karachi is the capital of Sindh province, and with just under fifteen million people recorded in our city registry, it ranks among the largest cities on Earth. It stands at 24.86 degrees north, 67 degrees east, on the northern shore of the Arabian Sea — and almost everything about the city flows from that position on the water: the port that handles the bulk of Pakistan's trade, the sea breeze that rescues its evenings, and the long beaches, Clifton and Sandspit and Hawksbay, where the whole city seems to gather at sunset.

Karachi's story runs in layers. It was a fishing settlement before it was a port, and a port before it was a metropolis. Under British rule it grew into one of the great harbours of the empire, its godowns and tramways serving the wheat and cotton of the Indus plain. At independence in 1947 it became the first capital of Pakistan — and its population multiplied as people arrived from every province and from across South Asia. That is why Karachi remains the most diverse city in the country: a dozen languages share its streets, and its food, its music, and its politics all carry that mixture.

Its central landmark is the Mazar-e-Quaid, the white marble mausoleum of Muhammad Ali Jinnah, the founder of Pakistan, rising from its gardens in the middle of the city. Around it the city spreads in rings: the carved stone of the colonial old town, the glass towers of the financial district around I. I. Chundrigar Road, the port cranes to the west, and the planned avenues of Clifton and Defence running down to the sea.
